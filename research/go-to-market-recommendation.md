# Go-to-market recommendation: an AI / automation startup in internal audit (India)

*Date: 14 Oct 2026. Builds on `internal-audit-large-companies-india.md` and `internal-audit-competitors-india.md`. This is a recommendation: the target segments are judgement calls based on that research, not market data.*

## Recommendation in one line

Start with a **narrow product: continuous tax and finance-controls audit** (GST/TDS reconciliation, ITC at risk from vendors who don't file, audit-trail exceptions). Sell it to **mid-to-large Indian companies with ₹500–5,000 cr turnover that operate across many states**. Reach them **directly and through mid-tier internal audit / CA firms**. Add banking clients (NBFCs) second.

---

## 1. Which market to target first

### Primary: mid-to-large corporates, ₹500–5,000 cr turnover, operating in many states

**Why this segment:**
- **High pain.** They have many state GSTINs, thousands of vendors and automated GST notices. 80% of enterprises received a notice in FY25, mostly from internal data mismatch. They also carry the audit-trail obligation and the new IT Act 2025 re-mapping. See the earlier report.
- **Under-served.** They are too small for MetricStream, SAP GRC or full Big 4 programmes. Their internal audit team is usually 2–10 people, often co-sourced with a CA firm and working in Excel.
- **Fast decisions.** The CFO can sign, so sales cycles are shorter than at Nifty-50 companies.
- **Large enough.** About 10,000–15,000 companies are in this band (Tier 2 plus the lower end of Tier 1 in the TAM model).

**Sectors to prioritise** (many GSTINs, heavy vendor bases, high ITC exposure):

| Sector | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Manufacturing (auto components, chemicals, engineering, textiles) | Many plants and depots, large supplier bases, heavy ITC |
| FMCG / consumer durables / distribution | Multi-state stock transfers, schemes and discounts, GST rate changes after the 2025 GST 2.0 slab rationalisation |
| Pharma / healthcare | Multi-state operations, a history of classification and ITC issues |
| Retail chains, QSR and logistics | Many locations and high transaction volume |
| Infrastructure / EPC / real estate | Complex works-contract GST and heavy TDS |

**How to build the target list:** Use Screener.in or CMIE Prowess. Filter for listed companies with sales of ₹500–5,000 cr in the sectors above, which is roughly the mid-cap and small-cap universe. Add unlisted companies and MNC subsidiaries from MCA or Tracxn data.

### Secondary: regulated finance (NBFCs, HFCs, cooperative banks)

- RBI requires **risk-based internal audit (RBIA)** for **all deposit-taking NBFCs and HFCs**, non-deposit-taking NBFCs and HFCs with assets ≥ ₹5,000 cr, and **urban cooperative banks with assets ≥ ₹500 cr**. ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/economy-policy/rbi-makes-risk-based-internal-audit-mandatory-for-select-nbfcs-ucbs-121020302071_1.html), [Money9 on HFCs](https://www.money9.com/news/banking/rbi-extends-risk-based-internal-audit-system-to-housing-finance-companies-44660))
- Upside: the mandate is clear, and budgets are clear.
- Downside: eTHIC (NCS) already sells to SBI, Axis and many other banks. Security and vendor reviews are long. Treat this as phase 2, once you have references.

### Avoid for now

- **Nifty-100 / very large companies.** Big 4 and SAP/Oracle are entrenched there, sales cycles run 9–18 months and procurement is heavy.
- **Companies under ₹200 cr.** Internal audit isn't mandatory for them and budgets are small. Consumer tax filing is crowded and commoditised (Clear, the free government portal).

## 2. Channel: sell through internal audit firms as well as directly

Around half of large Indian companies outsource or co-source internal audit. CA firms are the preferred provider ([ICMAI study](https://www.icmai.in/upload/AdvStudies/Executive-Summary-IA.pdf)).

- **Mid-tier firms:** Grant Thornton Bharat, BDO India, Protiviti India, PKC and similar. Also regional CA firms in Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Bengaluru, Chennai, Pune, Ahmedabad and Kolkata that run outsourced internal audit.
- **Pitch:** "Run full-population GST, TDS and audit-trail testing for all your internal audit clients with the same team." One firm can bring 20–50 client companies.
- **Big 4:** they have their own platforms (Omnia, Aura/Halo, Canvas/Helix, Clara). Approach them later, as a partner for India-specific tax analytics.

## 3. Who to contact

| Role | Why they buy |
|------|-------------|
| **CFO** | Owns the notice and demand risk and the budget |
| **Head of Internal Audit / Chief Audit Executive** | Primary user. Needs more coverage with a small team. |
| **Head of Indirect Tax / Tax Manager** | Feels the GST and ITC pain every month |
| **Audit Committee chair / independent directors** | Influencers who want assurance on tax risk |
| **Risk-advisory partner at an internal audit / CA firm** | Channel buyer |

**Where to find them:**
- IIA India chapters
- ICAI branch seminars (especially indirect tax and audit-trail sessions)
- CII and FICCI tax conferences
- CFO forums
- LinkedIn, searching titles like "Head Internal Audit", "GM Indirect Tax" and "CFO" at the target companies
- Introductions from CA firms

## 4. Wedge product and validation plan

1. **Wedge (first product):** an "ITC-at-risk and GST/TDS mismatch monitor". It connects to Tally, SAP or Oracle and to GSTR-2B data. It flags non-filing vendors, mismatches between GSTR-1, 3B and the books, and edits found in the audit trail. Output is audit-ready working papers plus a one-page summary for the audit committee.
   - **Why this wedge:** the ROI is in rupees (blocked ITC, notices avoided), so it is easy to justify.
   - **Expansion path:** once you have data access, add other modules: procure-to-pay, order-to-cash, payroll and fixed-asset controls, then Companies Act and CARO checks.
2. **Discovery:** hold 15–20 conversations (CFOs, CAEs, tax heads and 3–5 internal audit firm partners) to confirm the pain and willingness to pay.
3. **Paid pilots:** run 3–5 paid pilots of 4–6 weeks, at ₹3–5 lakh each, on one quarter of real data. Measure ₹ of ITC at risk found and notices pre-empted.
4. **Pricing hypothesis:** about ₹10–25 lakh ACV for mid-market companies. For audit firms, charge per client entity.
5. **Trust requirements:** India data residency, ISO 27001, and SOC 2 later. Show that Indian tax rules are encoded correctly, including mapping between the IT Act 2025 and the 1961 Act.

## 5. Risks

- **ERP data access and integration effort**, especially with heavily customised SAP setups.
- **Tax-compliance vendors** (Clear, Masters India, IRIS) could add audit features. Be clearly positioned as *independent assurance*, not filing.
- **Big 4 bundling** at the top end.
- Indian corporate buyers are price-sensitive.

## 6. Which sector to target first (added 14 Oct 2026)

*These scores are judgement calls, not market data. Validate them in customer interviews.*

Each sector is scored 1–5 (5 is best) on: how many audit areas it has (plants, inventory, vendors, locations); regulatory pressure; how many companies fall in the ₹500–5,000 cr band; how easy its data is to access (ERP maturity); and budget and speed of buying.

| Sector | Audit intensity | Regulatory pressure | Mid-large company count | Data access | Budget / speed | **Total** | Notes |
|--------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|-------|
| **Auto & auto components** | 5 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4 | **21** | Multi-plant. Heavy P2P (thousands of suppliers), inventory, scrap, job-work and GST stock transfers. Mostly SAP/Oracle. Clusters in Pune, Chennai, NCR and Gujarat. |
| **Pharma & medical devices** | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **22** | Plants plus distributors. Inventory, batch and expiry write-offs, regulator-driven controls (USFDA, CDSCO), trade schemes and discounts. Good margins mean budget. Clusters in Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Mumbai and Baddi. |
| **Hospitals / healthcare chains** | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | **18** | Many locations, billing and revenue leakage, insurance and TPA claims, pharmacy inventory, doctor payouts. Fewer companies in the band and messier hospital systems. |
| Chemicals / engineering / capital goods | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 17 | Similar to auto, with more Tally and older ERPs |
| FMCG / consumer durables | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 18 | Distribution, schemes, multi-state GST. Larger players are often Big 4 or SAP-locked. |
| NBFCs / HFCs | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 19 | RBIA mandate, but specialised (loan audits) and eTHIC competes |
| Retail / QSR chains | 5 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 16 | Store audits and cash. Thin margins. |
| **Software / IT services** | 2 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 3 | **15** | Little inventory or plant complexity. Internal audit is mostly payroll, contracts, revenue recognition and ITGC. Many are MNC or US-listed and already use SOX tools. |

### Recommendation

1. **Start with pharma and auto components together.** Both are manufacturing, so one product core works for both: P2P, inventory, O2C, R2R, payroll, fixed assets and GST. Both have many mandated companies in the ₹500–5,000 cr band, with SAP/Oracle data and real budgets. Pick **one city cluster** (for example Pune for auto, or Hyderabad/Ahmedabad for pharma) to win references fast.
2. **Next: hospital chains.** Add healthcare modules (billing leakage, TPA claims, pharmacy stock, doctor payouts). Pharma references help here.
3. **Later: NBFCs/HFCs, then FMCG and retail.**
4. **Deprioritise software / IT services.** The pain is low, they already have SOX tools, and the audit universe is small.

**First 20 discovery calls:** about 8 pharma companies, 8 auto component companies, and 4 internal audit / CA firms that serve these sectors.

## 7. Constraint: the startup does not want to do physical audits (added 14 Oct 2026)

**Key point:** the startup sells **software**, not audit services. Physical work (stock counts, plant and branch visits, asset tagging) stays with the customer's in-house internal audit team or its outsourced CA firm, as it does today. Most internal audit work is **data and document work**, and that is the part the product automates.

| Audit area in pharma / auto | Physical work needed? | Who does it | What the product does |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------|-------------|------------------------|
| P2P, O2C, R2R, payroll, GST/TDS, treasury | No, fully data and document based | Product (automated) | Full-population tests, document AI, working papers |
| ITGC / SoD / audit trail | No | Product | Access and edit-log analytics |
| Inventory | Partly: the count itself is physical | Customer's staff or CA firm counts | Imports count sheets, reconciles them to the books, flags variances, and analyses slow-moving, expired and write-off stock |
| Fixed assets | Partly: tag verification | Customer or CA firm | Reconciles the asset register, checks capitalisation and depreciation |
| Plant / branch operational audits | Yes | Customer or CA firm | Mobile checklist with photo and geo-tag evidence, which flows into the same report |

By judgement, roughly **75–85% of internal audit effort** in a manufacturing company is data and document work the product can cover. The physical 15–25% stays with people.

**If you want minimal physical exposure even in customer conversations,** these sectors are most remote-auditable:
- NBFCs / HFCs and fintech lenders (loan files and systems)
- Insurance
- E-commerce / D2C and new-age tech companies
- Services companies

The trade-off: these have less audit pain (IT services) or stronger incumbents (BFSI). Recommendation: keep pharma and auto as the first sectors, scope the product as **"remote / desk audit automation"**, and partner with CA firms for any physical component.
